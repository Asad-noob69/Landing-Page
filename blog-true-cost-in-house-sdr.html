The True Cost of Hiring an In-House SDR (And What It Really Buys You)

Revenue Ops · 7 min read

The salary is the number everyone quotes, and it is the smallest part of the story. When you add up everything it actually takes to put a productive SDR in a seat, the real cost is often close to double the base pay. Understanding that full picture is the only way to make a fair build-versus-partner decision.

The costs everyone counts

Base salary and commission are the obvious line items. A ramped SDR expects a competitive package, and on-target earnings climb quickly in a tight market. That number alone can make outbound feel expensive, but it is only the visible tip.

The costs most companies miss

Beneath the salary sits a stack of expenses that rarely make it into the plan:

  • Payroll taxes, benefits, and equipment, which add a meaningful percentage on top of base.
  • Tooling: CRM seats, sales engagement software, data and enrichment, dialers, and deliverability tools.
  • Recruiting and onboarding, whether that is agency fees or your team's time to hire.
  • Management overhead, because someone experienced has to coach, review calls, and build playbooks.
  • Ramp time, the three to six months of salary you pay before the rep produces meaningful pipeline.
By the time you count tooling, management, and ramp, the fully loaded cost of one in-house SDR often approaches twice the base salary.

The biggest hidden cost: risk

SDR turnover is famously high. If a rep leaves at month nine, you do not just lose them, you lose the ramp investment and start the clock over. That churn risk is real money, and it lands hardest on small teams that cannot absorb a gap in pipeline.

What that spend actually buys you

Here is the part that stings: for the fully loaded cost of a single in-house rep, you get one person, wearing one hat, who needs a manager, a tool stack, and half a year to ramp. If they underperform or quit, you carry the loss. That is a lot of concentration risk for one seat.

How a dedicated partner changes the math

An outsourced team spreads those fixed costs across a shared model. The tooling, management, coaching, and playbooks are already in place, so you pay for output instead of overhead. You get a team rather than a single point of failure, you launch in weeks instead of months, and if performance dips, it is the provider's job to fix it, not yours to re-hire.

The bottom line

In-house SDRs can absolutely be the right call, especially at scale. But compare honestly. Put the fully loaded cost, including tooling, management, ramp, and turnover risk, next to what a dedicated partner would charge for the same pipeline. Once the hidden costs are on the table, the decision usually looks very different.
